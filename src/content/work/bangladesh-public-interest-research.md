A public question often looks simple until the records are assembled. A change in the law is not the same as its enforcement. A budget allocation is not proof that a service reached the people it was meant for. And an allegation, however often repeated, does not become a finding through circulation.

This programme organises research on Bangladesh around those distinctions. Each piece of work starts from a question that identifiable evidence can answer — and states clearly what the evidence could **not** establish.

## Method

- A **chronology** of the relevant decisions and events.
- A **source register** recording official figures with their dates, definitions and denominators.
- A **map of responsibility**: which institutions made or carried out each decision.
- The **strongest competing explanation**, investigated rather than dismissed.

Material allegations are attributed, and the people or organisations concerned are given a real opportunity to respond.

## What gets published

Finished, checked essays and documentary episodes are published here with source notes and a correction history. Working files, unreviewed drafts and anything that could identify research participants stay private.

The free [evidence ledger](/workshop/evidence-ledger/) used in this work is available in the workshop.
