There is a style of public argument in which the conclusion arrives before the evidence. The speaker declares that an institution has failed, that a decision was inevitable, or that a statistic reveals everything. The language may be vivid, but the reader is being asked to share a certainty the author has not yet earned.

The alternative is not timid prose. It is to arrange the story so that the strongest sentence comes at the point where the records justify it.

## The document before the judgement

A public report can establish that an agency reported a number. It does not always establish why that number changed. An official instruction can establish what the law asked people to do; it does not establish how every office behaved.

## Writing for an ordinary reader

Define an unfamiliar term before showing its consequences. Introduce the institution before describing its powers. Place each number beside the source and period it measures. Use a short sentence for a conclusion only once the material for it is in view.
